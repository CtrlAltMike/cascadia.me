Keep going · Communication and reconnecting

Plan for Separation, Not Constant Contact.

Most households are apart for much of the day. The useful communication plan begins there: who carries the message, where people go, and what “I’m safe” looks like when a call does not connect.

Choose one person and three places.

Pick an out-of-area contact everyone can reach. Choose a meeting place near home, another outside the neighborhood, and one connected to school, work, care, or transit.

Write the plan on paper. Phones store excellent plans right up until the battery, network, passcode, or person holding the phone is unavailable.

Your first move

Send the out-of-area contact one message: “Will you be our check-in person?” Then make sure everyone knows the number.

Make each message useful.

Use a short format

Send name, time, location, status, destination, and next check-in. Separate confirmed facts from guesses. Text may work when a voice call does not.

Carry the plan

Keep a small card in wallets, backpacks, mobility equipment, school materials, and vehicles. Include communication needs and a way to identify the right support person.

Power the essentials

Keep charging cables, a power bank, vehicle adapter when available, hearing-aid batteries, and backup power for communication devices. Test them.

Know the official channel

Enroll in local alerts and identify the official emergency-management, weather, utility, school, work, and transit sources you would check first.

Make the plan usable by everyone.

Children and dependents

Decide who may pick someone up, how identity will be checked, and what happens if the usual caregiver or route is unavailable.

Communication access

Include plain-language, translated, visual, written, relay, captioned, or device-supported options. Keep a laminated communication board if it would help.

Privacy and safety

Do not publish household status, location, or vulnerability broadly. Choose who receives what and use a neutral message when disclosure could create risk.

Longer interruptions

Expect charging, network, mail, travel, and contact lists to fail unevenly. Use scheduled check-ins, physical message points, radio or public boards where established, and trusted runners.

Official instructions outrank the household schedule. Do not travel to a meeting place through an evacuation area, damaged route, floodwater, fire, downed lines, or another current hazard.

Reconnection works better when the other person agreed.

A plan can be small: one contact, one message format, one nearby place, one distant place, and one next check-in time. Practice it on an ordinary day.
