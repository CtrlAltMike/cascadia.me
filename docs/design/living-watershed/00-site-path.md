# Living Watershed: An Illustrated Cascadian Reader

> **Historical naming note (July 2026):** ReadyPlan.me was renamed NowWePlan.com. References to ReadyPlan in this dated design record are preserved as historical context; active site hooks and links use NowWePlan.

**Status:** Approved strategic direction; Phases 1–8 complete

**Date:** July 12, 2026

Living Watershed is the visual and editorial direction for Cascadia.me. It is not merely a theme and it is not an emergency-management interface dressed in regional art. It is an illustrated Cascadian reader with live instruments and a household workbook.

The artwork invites people into the place. The prose gives them a reason to remain. The evidence helps them decide what to do.

## The editorial constitution

> **Preparedness isn't about fear.**
>
> It's about care. You prepare because you have people to protect, a place worth defending, a life you want to keep. Every emergency kit is an act of imagination—a story about a future that hasn't happened yet, told by someone who decided to take it seriously.
>
> Most preparedness resources are either frightening or boring. Government brochures with all the warmth of a DMV waiting room. Survivalist forums that assume you want to live in a bunker. I built this site because I wanted something that treated me like an adult—honest about the risks, specific to where I live, and genuinely useful.
>
> Cascadia.me offers clear guidance for neighbors across the Cascadia bioregion, grounded in real geology, real weather, established safety guidance, and official public information—and candid about what those sources know, what they cannot know, and what still depends on where you live.

This belief governs the whole site. It is not a campaign line reserved for the homepage or a founder's note saved for the Approach page.

The final paragraph deliberately replaces the original claim of guidance based on “real experience.” Cascadia.me must not manufacture lived authority. Its humanity comes from truthful authorship, recognizable regional life, and clearly disclosed composite storytelling. Its factual authority comes from traceable public evidence.

## What Cascadia.me is

Cascadia.me speaks with the perspective of an informed Cascadian neighbor.

It is:

- Adult to adult, never paternalistic
- Regional without turning place into scenery
- Warm without pretending danger is harmless
- Useful without becoming a checklist factory
- Scientifically disciplined without borrowing the voice of an agency
- Honest about unequal resources, mobility, housing, transportation, caregiving, language, and isolation
- Motivated by care for people, place, animals, neighbors, and the continuity of ordinary life

It is not:

- A federal, state, provincial, or local emergency-management publication
- A disaster-news feed
- A survivalist manual
- A lifestyle brand
- A collection of shopping lists
- A gallery of disconnected slogans and calls to action
- A subscription funnel

Government agencies provide evidence, forecasts, warnings, and authoritative instructions. Cascadia.me provides regional context, interpretation, memory, and a humane path into action.

The evidence may come from institutions. The voice comes from here.

## The form

The closest editorial model is a classic illustrated regional reference book: substantial prose, large visual plates, explanatory diagrams, useful captions, memorable human narratives, and an authorial point of view.

That does not mean every page becomes an uninterrupted essay. It means prose is the spine. Illustration, live data, diagrams, safety summaries, references, and links support that spine instead of repeatedly replacing it.

### Reading path

A guide may move through:

**Place → People → Physical system → Changing conditions → Household decisions → Continuity and recovery**

This is a reading rhythm, not a reusable verbal template. Each hazard may choose a different sequence, pace, and emphasis.

### Reference path

Someone facing an event must still be able to reach:

- A concise “If this is happening now” safety summary
- Relevant live or official information
- The responsible local authority
- A practical action or household tool

The reference path remains easy to find, but it does not dictate the emotional or visual architecture of the entire chapter.

## Page roles

Each major area has its own editorial job.

### Homepage: the prologue

The homepage welcomes the reader into Cascadia as a connected place. It explains why the site exists, establishes preparedness as care, and introduces land, water, weather, and fire as parts of one regional story.

The guide pathways behave like an illustrated table of contents. The Atlas and preparation tools are meaningful destinations, not competing promotional buttons.

### Guides: individual chapters

Each guide should feel authored for its subject. The guides share standards of truth, safety, accessibility, and provenance; they do not share a stack of slogans, three-part bands, or identical section formulas.

The page should remain recognizable as Earthquake, Wildfire, Flooding, or Winter Storm even if its title is hidden.

### Atlas: the live regional instrument

The Atlas answers what connected sources are reporting, what models suggest, what happened before, and what a planning layer can show. It is more compact and operational than a guide because that is its purpose.

Its interface may be fragmented where controls require it. Its explanatory copy should remain ordinary, regional, and candid rather than clinical.

### Build Your Kit: the household workbook

Preparation is treated as an act of care and imagination, not an act of consumption. The capability model remains, joined by practical inventories, a communication plan, and printable tools that help different households make the guidance their own.

### The Approach: the author's note and evidence method

The Approach holds the fuller origin story, the site's relationship with the reader, its source and maintenance practices, its composite-story and illustration policies, its cultural boundaries, and the explicit promise that Cascadia.me supplements rather than replaces local emergency management.

### Guides index: the table of contents

The Guides page helps someone recognize which chapters belong to their place and household. It is not a card catalog or an action-routing dashboard.

## Human narrative without false reporting

Each principal guide may contain one substantial composite household narrative. The story exists to restore recognizable life: people, roads, pets, work, weather, neighbors, medication, power, distance, uncertainty, and imperfect decisions.

Use one quiet disclosure at the story's first appearance:

> **Composite account:** The people and scene are created to illustrate sourced regional conditions and preparedness decisions. They are not reports about identifiable residents or a specific emergency.

Composite narratives may use names, routines, relationships, animals, jobs, familiar roads, and recognizable regional settings. They may not:

- Resemble eyewitness reporting or testimony
- Invent quotations attributed to real people
- Use precise times, addresses, measurements, official orders, injuries, losses, or outcomes that imply a documented incident
- Turn one household's decision into universal guidance
- Use suffering merely to manufacture urgency
- Claim that a character survived or experienced a real event

Real incidents may be discussed when their facts and consequences are sourced and clearly separated from the composite story. Every safety decision inside a composite remains evidence-backed. Generated illustrations remain interpretive, never documentary.

Sensory detail is welcome when it builds recognition, attachment, or understanding. It should describe the rain on a roof, smoke held in a valley, a river changing sound, a dark road, a warm room, or the ordinary work of helping another person—not stage disaster as spectacle.

## Distinct chapter temperaments

The primary guides share standards, not catchphrases.

- **Earthquake** begins in ordinary life and is suddenly interrupted. Its voice is geological, intimate, and attentive to the first minutes.
- **Wildfire** carries season, dryness, distance, wind, smoke, and the difficult decision to leave before certainty arrives.
- **Flooding** follows accumulation, thresholds, familiar roads changing character, and water returning to old paths.
- **Winter Storm** is about darkness, isolation, warmth, fragile infrastructure, and neighbors helping one another endure a long interruption.

Repeated constructions across guides are a warning that the writing has become templated.

## Reading standards

- Connected paragraphs are the default.
- Headings mark genuine changes in subject; they do not summarize every small thought.
- A utility label, slogan, metric row, supporting line, and button should not be stacked merely to fill a section.
- A call to action appears only when the reader is genuinely changing tasks.
- Ordinary continuation is a text link or simply the next paragraph, not a button.
- Metadata belongs in a quiet field note, caption, or source apparatus unless it is time-critical.
- Practical guidance may live inside prose, diagrams, captions, and a concise reference summary.
- Before / During / After may remain as a reference tool when useful. It is not the mandatory narrative spine.
- A three-part action sequence is used only when the subject genuinely supports it or official guidance establishes it. Drop / Cover / Hold On remains; other guides do not imitate it automatically.
- A beautiful phrase may invite attention, but the reader should never have to decode the literal guidance beneath it.
- Calm is an emotional stance, not a short-copy rule.

- Heroes establish place, scale, and emotional weather.
- Large plates allow the reader to pause and look.
- Process illustrations reveal physical systems.
- Household scenes carry human narratives.
- Captions reward attention and continue the surrounding prose.

Editorial reconstruction may change placement, scale, responsive presentation, caption treatment, and surrounding prose. It may not remove, replace, regenerate, recolor, redraw, or materially alter an existing work without explicit owner approval.

## Truth, safety, and accessibility

The factual and safety gains of the first Living Watershed implementation remain non-negotiable:

- Distinguish live observation, forecast, official order, historical fact, planning layer, and composite story. Label artwork only when a reasonable reader could mistake it for a live map, official data product, or documentary record.
- Source consequential claims and display review dates.
- Never convert missing, stale, partial, or unavailable data into reassurance.
- Explain uncertainty where it changes a decision.
- Let local authorities, routes, maps, utilities, and conditions override regional generalization.
- Keep life-safety guidance and official source links public.
- Include disability, medical power, mobility, transportation, renters, animals, caregiving, cost, language, and isolation in the main guidance rather than an appendix.
- Offer workable alternatives when the ideal action requires money, ownership, physical ability, or a vehicle.
- Never moralize about readiness, evacuation, poverty, disability, housing, or recovery.
- Preserve relevant U.S., Canadian, provincial, state, Tribal, First Nation, county, regional-district, and local distinctions.
- Preserve cultural integrity and never claim Indigenous authority or invent Indigenous visual language.

These standards are the book's invisible scaffolding. They should make the writing trustworthy without making it sound institutional.

## ReadyPlan's future role

The separation remains clean:

- **Cascadia.me:** public knowledge, live conditions, immediate safety, regional understanding, and freely usable household guidance
- **ReadyPlan.me:** private household plans, assignments, reminders, progress, and subscription tools

Core life-safety information never sits behind a ReadyPlan subscription. The revised pages may prepare natural transition points, but they do not display empty widgets, implementation terminology, or promotional interruptions before the modules exist.

## Editorial test

Before a page is complete, ask:

- Does it sound as though it was written here?
- Does care—not fear, compliance, or consumption—motivate the reader?
- Can someone settle into the page and remain immersed?
- Can the reader move through several connected paragraphs without being reset by a card, slogan, metric strip, or call to action?
- Does each illustration deepen the passage before and after it?
- Is urgent action still easy to reach?
- Is any poetic phrase less clear than the literal truth beneath it?
- Could any composite detail be mistaken for reporting?
- Does each person retain dignity and agency?
- Could the chapter be identified without seeing its title?
- Does every interruption justify the reader's lost attention?
- Has every assigned Living Watershed artwork been preserved?
- Could the prose appear unchanged in a federal brochure? If so, it is not finished.

The identity is no longer a utility slogan. It is the founding belief:

> **Preparedness isn't about fear. It's about care.**
