# Living Watershed: Completing the Book

> **Historical naming note (July 2026):** ReadyPlan.me was renamed NowWePlan.com. References to ReadyPlan in this dated design record are preserved as historical context; active site hooks and links use NowWePlan.

**Status:** Phase 7 complete

**Revision status:** Version 0.7 production record

**Date:** July 11, 2026

**Scope:** Guides, Approach, shared wayfinding, and lightweight utility surfaces

Phase 7 completes the book around the finished homepage prologue, four primary chapters, household workbook, and Regional Hazard Atlas. It removes the remaining orientation-dashboard and institutional-trust-page patterns without inventing new hazard pages, duplicating live conditions, or entering the Phase 8 complete-site audit.

## Guides as the table of contents

`guides.html` is now a continuous illustrated table of contents rather than a decision dashboard.

- The locked connected-landscape composition remains the full-width frontispiece at its intrinsic 2:1 ratio.
- The urgent “recognize the signal” framing is replaced by a calm explanation of why four chapters carry different clocks.
- Land, Water, Weather, and Fire mini-panels are removed; the illustration and one connected sentence carry that relationship.
- Earthquake, Wildfire, Flooding, and Winter Storm appear as real headings in a semantic ordered table of contents.
- Each chapter receives the substantial place-and-household summary established on the homepage rather than repeated Signal / First decision fragments.
- Every existing ReadyPlan hazard and capability hook remains on its explicit chapter link.
- Current conditions remain in the Atlas. The closing coda quietly routes readers to the Atlas for changing conditions and Build Your Kit for household work.

The result reads like a guidebook frontispiece and contents sequence: one strong image, sustained regional copy, generous typography, and thin chapter rules. It avoids cards, badges, alarm language, and a CTA funnel.

## Approach as the author’s note and evidence method

`approach.html` now introduces the author-reader relationship before the trust machinery.

The opening establishes:

- Preparedness as an act of care
- Michael Hendrick’s 2016 family-kit origin in Washington
- The informed-neighbor intention behind Cascadia.me
- The explicit boundary that the author is not a seismologist, fire scientist, meteorologist, or emergency manager
- An accessible Pulitzer reference instead of sending readers directly to a subscriber-gated article

Observe / Interpret / Decide remains as the page’s one compact evidence instrument. The former source ledger, promise grid, illustration disclaimer, and ReadyPlan comparison module are replaced by connected prose covering:

- Primary and traceable source selection
- Review dates, source clocks, and geographic coverage
- Observation, forecast, historical/planning, and official-instruction distinctions
- The rule that failed or partial data never becomes an all-clear
- Mountain-wind, perimeter, and planning-layer limitations
- Local authority for current protective instructions
- Composite household passages without fabricated lived experience
- Cultural integrity and no borrowed Indigenous authority or decoration
- Editorial independence, no advertising or affiliate links, and the public Cascadia.me / private ReadyPlan boundary

A quiet source appendix supplies the four information classes, representative source families, authorship, and review date. No routine site copy explains that illustrations are illustrations.
