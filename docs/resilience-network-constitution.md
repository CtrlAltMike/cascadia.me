# The Resilience Network Constitution

**Version 1.0 — adopted July 25, 2026**

This document governs NowWePlan.com, Cascadia.me, HurricaneCoast.me, and SanAndreas.me.

It is short on purpose. Every clause here is binding, and every clause here is one that will, at some point, prevent us from doing something we want to do. That is what makes it a constitution rather than a description of our taste. Guidance that shapes the work without constraining it belongs in the Editorial Handbook, which is subordinate to this document and may be revised freely.

## Purpose

The network exists to help ordinary people make the lives they already have less brittle: to understand the places where they live, prepare for interruptions without organizing their lives around fear, recover faster when systems fail, and build the relationships that make recovery possible.

**The promise: Understand what your place can do. Make the life you have less brittle. Know who you will face it with.**

## Articles

1. **Consequences before causes.** Hazards differ physically; their domestic consequences overlap almost entirely. The network organizes preparation around recurring consequences—water, sanitation, temperature, air, light, information, food, medication, money, documents, movement, dependents, staying, leaving, reconnecting. Hazard-specific content is reserved for the places where specificity genuinely matters: the first minutes, the building, the route, and the local authority.

2. **Accuracy in both directions.** We do not intensify risk for attention, and we do not soften it for comfort. We publish delay, duration, and difficulty honestly—including that organized assistance to a large region arrives in days to weeks, and that recovery outlasts response by months or years. We do not claim that help will not come, and we do not imply that it will come quickly. Uncertainty is stated as uncertainty; it is never used as a reason to withhold a useful conclusion.

3. **No shame.** We never direct shame, contempt, or condescension at unprepared households, and we do not use fear, guilt, urgency, or scarcity as instruments of persuasion. Urgency may arise only from the genuine usefulness and timing of an action.

4. **No transfer of institutional failure.** We do not make households responsible for unsafe buildings, fragile infrastructure, poverty, inadequate transit, or failed public systems. Where no meaningful individual action exists, we say so plainly and name the collective or institutional intervention that would matter instead.

5. **Unequal capacity is assumed, not appended.** Advice written for a homeowner with a garage, a vehicle, a flexible job, and disposable income is never presented as universal. Where private capacity is limited, the community and public layers become more important, not less.

6. **The authority boundary.** Official agencies issue alerts, orders, forecasts, and emergency instructions; qualified professionals assess buildings, health, and insurance. We help readers find, understand, and act on those sources. We never impersonate them, and we never issue remote diagnoses of specific buildings or situations.

7. **Live conditions override us.** When an event is active or imminent in a region we serve, current official instruction takes precedence over everything we have published. The affected site must say so prominently and route readers to the responsible authority before offering any content of its own.

8. **Money never moves editorial.** No commercial relationship—affiliate, sponsorship, partnership, or sale—may influence what we recommend, how we rank it, or what we choose to cover. Any such relationship is disclosed at the point of recommendation. We do not treat purchased equipment as a measure of preparedness or competence.

9. **Trust before conversion.** Each regional site is complete and useful on its own; none may be weakened into an advertisement for NowWePlan. No private household information passes from a regional site to NowWePlan, or to any third party, without the reader’s explicit and informed action.

10. **Reachability.** Content must remain usable by the people most likely to need it: legible on old devices and poor connections, printable, and compatible with assistive technology. A page that cannot be read during an outage has failed at the thing it exists to do.

11. **Currency.** Every substantive page carries a visible review date. Any content pointing to official sources, routes, or instructions is verified on a fixed schedule. Material errors are corrected openly, in place, and noted.

12. **Fiction is labeled.** Every fictional work is identified as fiction. Composite circumstances are never presented as testimony, reporting, or firsthand account. Safety-relevant content within a story is grounded in credible evidence, with factual notes kept visibly separate from the narrative. Suffering is never the attraction.

## The governing test

Before publishing any page, feature, story, illustration, product, or campaign:

1. Does this help someone understand their place or preserve ordinary life?
2. Does it clarify consequences, or merely dramatize causes?
3. Does it increase agency without manufacturing fear?
4. Does it hold up for a reader with less money, less mobility, or less time than we assumed?
5. Does it strengthen connection rather than isolation?
6. Are the authority and evidence boundaries visible to the reader?
7. Does it help shorten the recovery, not only survive the event?
8. Can the reader act on it and then set the subject down?

Work that fails these questions does not belong in the network, regardless of how well it would perform.

## Amendment and authority

This constitution is maintained by Michael Hendrick, who is currently its sole author and sole decider. That is stated plainly rather than obscured behind institutional language.

Amendments are versioned, dated, and logged publicly with a one-line rationale. The Editorial Handbook may change at any time without amendment. Where the Handbook and this document conflict, this document governs.

## Why these rules exist

Serious risk can be held without obsession. Preparation can be practical without becoming tactical, communal without becoming sentimental, and honest without becoming alarmist.

The desired outcome for a reader is not vigilance. It is earned calm—and the strongest household is not the one that imagines it needs nobody, but the one that understands its dependencies, protects what it can, and participates in a living network of reciprocal care.

These twelve articles exist because every one of them will eventually cost us something, and we would rather decide now, while it is cheap.
