Keep Life Going

Start with what can't safely stop.

Water from the tap. A working toilet. Medication kept at the right temperature. A way to breathe, move, communicate, and care for the people and animals who depend on you.

Start with water Use the household workbook

Ordinary functions

A list of equipment is not a plan.

Each subject begins with the function, the life it serves, and the constraints a household actually has.

Water

Where will it go? Can you lift it? What happens when storage runs out or local water becomes unsafe?

Keep water available
Sanitation

A toilet is part of a system.

Plan for hygiene, waste, and handwashing when water or wastewater service is interrupted.

Protect sanitation
Temperature

Keep people safely warm or cool.

Start with the room, clothing, shade, safe heat, sleep, power needs, and the place you could go.

Keep a safe temperature
Air

Make one room easier to breathe in.

Smoke, ash, heat, cold, generators, and building systems can all change indoor air.

Protect indoor air
Food and medication

Know what needs cold, power, timing, or replacement.

Write down medication names, doses, and prescribers. “The small white one” is a poor description.

Protect food and medication
Communication

Plan for separation, not constant contact.

Choose contacts, meeting places, and a low-bandwidth way to say where you are.

Plan communication and reconnection
Money and documents

Make the next office easier.

Keep identity, insurance, account, property, and medical information findable without carrying everything.

Make records findable
Movement

Fit the route to the person.

Time, mobility, transit, fuel, children, animals, weather, and a locked building can all change the same departure.

Fit the route to the person
Staying or leaving

Prepare for both until one is clearly right.

Current orders and conditions decide the moment. Earlier arrangements make either choice less difficult.

Prepare both paths
Light and information

Keep one light and one trusted source working.

Use backup power where it changes a decision, and know which official source you will check next.

Keep light and information available
Animals and dependents

Keep care going when the routine moves.

Make food, medicine, equipment, authority, transport, and one willing backup person part of the plan.

Make care portable

Fit matters

Use the life you already have.

A renter cannot change every building system. A household without a vehicle cannot use a vehicle-based plan. Someone who depends on powered medical equipment needs more than a reminder to charge a phone. Storage, money, health, work, language, caregiving, transport, and trust belong in the main guidance.

Begin with one useful move. A few bottles will not cover a long outage, but they are more useful than an ideal supply you have nowhere to keep.
