Recovery

Ordinary problems, made harder.

Finding a place to stay. Reaching work. Replacing medication. Documenting damage. Telling the same story to another office. You cannot solve all of that in advance. You may be able to remove one or two complications.

Before the paperwork starts

Protect the choices you may need later.

Safety and current instructions come first. When there is room, record enough to make the next conversation clearer.

  1. Keep people and medication reachable.

    Write down where household members are staying, how to reach them, what medical care cannot wait, and which pharmacy or prescriber may need to help.

  2. Photograph what you can see safely.

    Capture the wider scene and the details before cleanup or temporary repair. Do not enter an unsafe building to improve the record.

  3. Start one running log.

    Record dates, names, claim or case numbers, promises, deadlines, expenses, and where documents were sent. A notebook is enough.

  4. Keep receipts and temporary-repair records.

    Save lodging, transport, medication, food, materials, and other event-related costs. Coverage and assistance rules vary, but missing records rarely help.

  5. Check the source before paying or signing.

    Contractor licensing, insurance, disaster assistance, tenant rights, and public programs differ by location. Use the responsible agency and take time when the decision can wait.

Different clocks

Recovery does not move as one line.

A utility may return before a household does. School may reopen while transport remains difficult. A claim may still be open after the public story has moved on.

Housing

Where can you stay, and for how long?

Keep lease, mortgage, insurance, inspection, landlord, and temporary-housing information together. Ask what is confirmed and what is only expected.

Work and school

What proof or arrangement will be needed?

Save closure notices, attendance expectations, employer contacts, caregiving constraints, and transport alternatives.

Medical continuity

What cannot wait for normal systems?

Prescriptions, powered equipment, refrigeration, dialysis, mobility, mental-health care, and home-health visits may need different routes.

Insurance and assistance

Which program owns the next decision?

Insurance, local relief, government assistance, loans, and nonprofit aid have different rules. Confirm deadlines and required records with the source.

Transport and access

What is open enough to use?

Road, ferry, transit, fuel, parking, elevators, accessible routes, and reentry rules may recover on different schedules.

Community

Who may not return?

Neighbors, tenants, workers, small businesses, health services, schools, and cultural institutions are part of recovery infrastructure too.

Find the current program, not a remembered one

Assistance programs and eligibility change by event and jurisdiction. Use Find Official Help and the current local or national disaster-assistance source. Cascadia.me does not determine eligibility or replace legal, insurance, medical, engineering, or financial advice.
